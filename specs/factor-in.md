# Factor In  (`factor-in`)

Status: live · Skill map: after Factor Out; before Conversions · Code: `exercises/factor-in.js` (+ `lib/places.js`)

## What the student learns
Rewrite scientific notation as a decimal by moving the point until the power is gone; each hop puts one 10 (or 10⁻¹) back into the number.

## The visual
"4.5 × 10³ =" above; the row starts as 4.5 with the power riding at its end. In the tutorial and the opening the power counts down live as the point moves (× 10³ → × 10² → … → gone); after that the student works it out from the question (no live power). After a wrong answer, the sentence names what's left over ("That leaves × 10¹").

## Ideas and kinds
| Idea | Easy | Tricky |
|---|---|---|
| pos (bigger) | 4.5 × 10¹ → 45 | 4.5 × 10³ → 4,500; 3.527 × 10² → 352.7 |
| neg (smaller) | 3.5 × 10⁻¹ → 0.35 | 3.5 × 10⁻³ → 0.0035; 4.08 × 10⁻² → 0.0408 |
| zero (late twist) | 6.2 × 10⁰ → 6.2 | same |

Opening: 4.5 × 10³ and 4.5 × 10⁻³ (live power). 7 questions with no misses.

## Feedback (error types)
| Error | Sentence |
|---|---|
| dir | × 10⁻³ makes it smaller, so the point moves left. (or bigger → right) |
| count | That leaves × 10¹. × 10² is 2 hops. |
| zero | 10⁰ is 1, so the point stays. |

Cue: the picked point, then the right move replays with a "10" going back into the number each hop and the power counting down to nothing; "= 4,500". Right answers wait for Next.

## Tutorial (2 steps)
"Drag the point until the power is gone." (4.5 × 10³) → "Now this one." (4.5 × 10⁻³).

## Cheat sheet
Rule: move the point as many places as the power (positive → right, negative → left). Two drawn examples (45 × 10³, 45 × 10⁻³).
