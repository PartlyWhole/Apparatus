## The thinking behind the test

Every question comes down to a few moves, and most of them are moves the student already knows from Module 4: run and rise, the 90° turn, the perpendicular.

## The shared visual: the grid, and the point's own triangle

The anchor picture stays the run-and-rise triangle, now drawn from a center to a point:

- **translation:** the vector is a run and a rise, the same blue and green legs, drawn from every vertex to its image;
- **rotation about the origin:** the triangle from the origin to the point (run x, rise y) **turns 90°** — the same turn as Module 4's perpendicular — so (x, y) lands on (−y, x). The rule is shown, not told. 180° is two turns; 270° (or 90° clockwise) is three;
- **reflection:** a dashed segment from the point straight to the mirror line and the same length past it, with equal tick marks; the line is the perpendicular bisector;
- **symmetry:** the figure with its lines of symmetry dashed and a ghost copy turning onto itself.

Grid conventions carry over: only letters on the grid (A, A′), coordinates in the legend, run blue and rise green, small numbers, the answer box where "/" makes a fraction.

## Exercise details

### T1 Vectors and Translations
- **Ideas:** read a vector (arrow on the grid → ⟨a, b⟩; point and image → image − preimage); apply it (point + vector; a figure, by tapping image vertices); words → vector (compass directions, a route of blocks, late); backwards (image − vector = preimage, late).
- **Scaffold → fade:** at first the vector's run and rise legs are drawn (blue, green) and the student types ⟨a, b⟩; then only the arrow; then only coordinates. Applying: first "A(2, 3) + ⟨4, −1⟩ = (□, □)" with the legs drawn from A; then plotting a whole triangle by tapping its image vertices; then numbers only.
- **Feedback:** reversed: "From A to A′ is 8 right and 1 down: ⟨8, −1⟩." · swapped: "The across part comes first: ⟨8, −1⟩." · sign: "Left is negative: ⟨−3, 2⟩." · northeast picked as ⟨500, 0⟩: "Northeast is both east and north: ⟨500, 500⟩."
- **Answers:** vector boxes ⟨□, □⟩ and point boxes (□, □) (the shared answer box); tap-to-plot on the grid.

### T2 Rotate About the Origin
- **Tutorial:** P(4, 1). Its triangle from the origin (run 4, rise 1) turns 90° counterclockwise; it lands with run −1, rise 4: P′(−1, 4). "The run and rise swap, and the new run changes sign: (x, y) → (−y, x)." A second turn gives 180°: (−4, −1).
- **Ideas:** 90° counterclockwise; 180°; 270° counterclockwise / 90° clockwise (the same turn, two names); a figure (tap or type each vertex); what a rotation keeps (true/false facts: a vertex and its image are the same distance from the center; angles at the center are equal; a vertex's distance to another vertex is not the center distance).
- **Feedback:** (y, x) for 90°: "That's a flip across y = x. A turn also changes a sign: (−y, x)." · clockwise used as counterclockwise: "Clockwise turns the other way: 90° clockwise is (y, −x)."
- **Answers:** point boxes; tap the image point; for the facts, True / False.

### T3 How Far It Turned
- Minute hand from 10:05 to 10:42 (37 minutes × 6° = 222°, clockwise); a restaurant that turns once an hour (40 minutes → 240°); a wheel with 36 cars (10° between); a regular 15-gon (24°).
- Feedback: "Each minute is 6°: 37 × 6 = 222°." · interior angle given: "The angle of symmetry is 360° ÷ 15 = 24°, not the corner angle."
- Short (about 5 answers); could fold into T2 and T7 as late twists instead of its own page (open question 3).

### T4 Reflect It
- **Visual:** the mirror line drawn; the point's dashed perpendicular to the line with its length in the legend; the image the same distance past, equal ticks.
- **Ideas:** across the x-axis / y-axis; across y = x / y = −x; across x = a / y = b (count the distance to the line, go as far past: x → 2a − x); a figure (tap or type).
- **Feedback:** wrong axis: "Across the x-axis the x stays: (7, −5)." · y = x for y = −x: "Across y = −x the numbers swap and both change sign: (−5, −7)." · axis instead of x = −2: "The mirror is x = −2: 4 to the right of it lands 4 to the left of it, at x = −6."

### T5 Name the Transformation
- **Steps (scaffold):** Is the image facing the same way (same order of vertices around)? No → a reflection: which line? Yes → a slide or a turn: is every vertex moved by the same vector? If so, a translation; if not, a rotation: what angle, which way?
- Fades to the test's format: four named choices.
- **Feedback:** "A reflection reverses the order of the vertices; here it's kept: a rotation." · "270° clockwise is the same as 90° counterclockwise: W(−7, 5) → (−5, −7)."

### T6 Two in a Row
- Reflect across x = −2, then the x-axis (the test's Q9); reflect across two parallel lines (→ a translation, twice the gap); across both axes (→ a 180° turn); translate then rotate (Q12-style, one vertex per part).
- The first image is drawn faint before the second is plotted; a final ›-stepped "what one move does both?" (house rule: one idea per step, student-paced).

### T7 Symmetry
- **By eye first** (as in Classify the Quadrilateral): a shape, "How many lines of symmetry?" and "Smallest turn that maps it onto itself?" with the lines and the turning ghost shown after a miss.
- **Then by count:** regular n-gons (n lines, 360°/n), composite figures (a square with four triangles: 4 lines, 90°), figures with rotation but no lines (a pinwheel), lines but no rotation (a kite).
- **Backwards (late):** "A regular polygon's angle of symmetry is 24°: how many sides?"

## Answer formats (new building blocks)
- **Vector** ⟨□, □⟩: two shared answer boxes in angle brackets.
- **Tap to plot:** tap lattice points for the image's vertices in order; they join up; tap again to undo (a grid addition, like drawLine).
- **Mirror line** drawn on the grid for x = a, y = b, y = x, y = −x.
- **True / False** and named choices: lib/pick.js.

## Open questions
1. **Clockwise or counterclockwise first?** The book's default is counterclockwise; the test asks clockwise (Q3, Q12C). Recommendation: teach counterclockwise first, then clockwise as "the other way", and name both on the 270° items.
2. **Plotting by tapping** (vs typing coordinates) for whole figures. Recommendation: type a single point first; tap to plot whole figures (as the test's Q9 asks to graph); the numbers stay in the legend.
3. **T3 as its own page** or as late twists in T2 (clock) and T7 (360°/n). Recommendation: fold it in; it is two ideas with one formula each.
4. **Q10 (the compass construction)** is skipped as asked; the property it tests (each vertex moves the length of the vector) is a true/false fact in T1.
